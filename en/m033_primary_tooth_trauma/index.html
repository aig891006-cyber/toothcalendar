When Your Child Falls and Hurts a Baby Tooth: Knocked-Out and Loose Teeth

A hurt baby tooth is usually just watched, but a dental check is a must. Why a knocked-out baby tooth isn't put back, what to do, and when the ER comes first.

At a glance

  • Never put back a knocked-out baby tooth
  • Loose, pushed in, or moved? See a dentist ASAP
  • Passed out, vomiting, or very drowsy? ER first

Once kids start walking and running, falling and bumping the mouth is common. When there's blood and your child is crying, it's easy to freeze, not knowing what to do first. Here's what to do calmly when a baby tooth gets hurt, and the signs that mean you should head to the dentist or ER right away.

What can happen when a baby tooth gets hurt

Toddlers who are learning to walk and run often fall forward and hurt their upper front teeth. Injuries come in many forms. A tooth can get loose, be pushed up into the gum, be pushed forward or back out of position, or come out completely. A tooth can also chip, or the lips and gums can get cut.

Most baby-tooth injuries are simply watched while they heal on their own. Still, there's an important reason for a dental check. The permanent tooth bud sits right below the baby tooth. Even if things look fine on the outside, only a dentist can tell whether the root or the area underneath was hurt.

In the weeks after an injury, the tooth may turn gray, yellow, or pinkish. It's alarming, but discoloration alone isn't treated right away. Over weeks to months, it may fade back to its original color. Still, it needs to be watched, so show it to the dentist at every checkup.

Here are some things parents often mix up.

  • "The bleeding stopped and my child is playing, so it's fine": Even if it looks okay, you can't tell about the permanent tooth bud underneath. Have it checked by a dentist the same day or the next.
  • "If you bring the tooth in milk, they'll put it back": That's for permanent teeth. Baby teeth aren't put back.
  • "A gray tooth will fall out soon": Discoloration alone doesn't mean the tooth will fall out or needs treatment right away. Keep an eye out for other signs.

Here's what to do after a fall

  1. Check on your child first. If your child lost consciousness, is vomiting, or is unusually drowsy, a head injury comes before the tooth. Go to the emergency room right away.
  2. If there's bleeding, apply pressure. Press gently and steadily on the bleeding spot with clean gauze or a towel. The mouth can look like it's bleeding a lot, but pressure usually stops it.
  3. Look inside the mouth. Once your child calms down a bit, gently lift the upper lip and look at the front teeth and gums. Check whether a tooth is loose, pushed in, out of position, or missing.
  4. Find the tooth and bring it. A knocked-out baby tooth is never put back in. But it's a good idea to bring the tooth to the dentist, so they can confirm it came out completely and wasn't pushed up into the gum. If you can't find the tooth, be sure to mention that too.
  5. If a tooth is loose, pushed in, or out of position, see a dentist as soon as possible. For other injuries, have it checked by a dentist the same day or the next.
  6. Be gentle for a few days. Offer soft foods, and gently clean the hurt area with a soft toothbrush too. Keeping it clean helps it heal.

Putting a baby tooth back in can damage the permanent tooth bud growing right beneath it. Permanent teeth are handled completely differently, so keep the two straight.

If the tooth changes color, write down the date and take a photo. It helps the dentist follow how it's doing.

Frequently asked questions

Can't the knocked-out baby tooth be put back in?

Baby teeth aren't put back. Doing so could damage the permanent tooth bud right below it. Don't throw the tooth away. Bring it to the dentist to have it checked.

The tooth was pushed up into the gum. What do I do?

See a dentist as soon as possible. The dentist will check how far and in what direction it was pushed, and decide whether to watch it or do something else. Don't pull or press on the tooth at home.

The hurt front tooth turned gray. Does it need a root canal?

Discoloration alone doesn't mean a root canal right away. It may fade over time. But if a pimple-like bump appears on the gum, or there's swelling or pain, go back to the dentist.

Could this cause problems with the permanent tooth later?

The permanent tooth bud sits right below the baby tooth, so it needs to be watched for any effect. Sometimes you can't tell until the permanent tooth comes in, so keep showing the injured tooth to the dentist at regular checkups.

Get care right away if

  • Your child lost consciousness, is vomiting, or is unusually drowsy (ER first)
  • A tooth is loose, pushed up into the gum, or out of position
  • A tooth was knocked out completely, or you can't find the tooth
  • Bleeding doesn't stop well even with pressure
  • Later on, a pimple-like bump appears on the gum or the tooth keeps getting darker
