White or Yellow Patches on New Molars? Understanding MIH in Children

Patches with clear edges on new six-year molars or front teeth, especially if sensitive, may be MIH. Why it's not a brushing problem and how to care for it.

At a glance

  • Look for white, yellow, or brown patches with clear edges
  • It's not caused by poor brushing
  • Patchy teeth do better with check-ups every 3 to 6 months

Spotting white or yellowish patches on a brand-new molar can be worrying. "Is that a cavity already?" "Did I not brush well enough?" These patches may be a condition called MIH. Here's what it is, why it isn't your fault, and how to protect those teeth at home.

What is MIH?

MIH (molar-incisor hypomineralization) is a condition in which the outer layer, the enamel, of the first permanent molars (which come in around age 6) and the front teeth forms softer than normal. Teeth form inside the gums for a long time before they come into the mouth. During that process, the enamel didn't harden fully. That's why the patches are already there when the tooth comes in. It's not rare: roughly one child in ten has it.

Here's what it looks like:

  • White, yellow, or brown patches.
  • A clear edge between the patch and the normal part of the tooth.
  • It can affect just one molar or several, and how severe it is differs from tooth to tooth.

Softer enamel causes a few problems. The teeth are easily sensitive to cold water or cold air, the chewing surface chips easily, and cavities progress quickly. So the earlier you notice it, the easier it is to manage.

One thing to remember: MIH is not caused by poor brushing. It's already settled before the tooth comes in, so there's no reason to blame yourself. But because the tooth is weaker, the care after it comes in matters even more.

If you spot a patch, here's what to do

First, take a photo. The back molars are hard to see, so have your child open wide in good light. If a new molar or front tooth has uneven color, showing that photo to the dentist makes the visit easier. Taking another photo later is also a good way to compare changes over time.

Brushing tips for a child with sensitive teeth:

  1. Switch to a soft toothbrush.
  2. Use lukewarm water, not cold, for brushing.
  3. Brush twice a day with fluoride toothpaste. On the sensitive side, go slowly with a light touch.
  4. Do a final check to make sure your child isn't skipping that tooth.

It's common for kids to avoid brushing the sensitive side. But without brushing, plaque builds up, and cavities form faster on weak enamel. The key is not to skip brushing, even when it feels sensitive.

Look at food and drinks too. Sweets add to cavity risk, and acidic drinks wear away enamel. Cut back on how often your child snacks, and make water the everyday drink.

If the dentist recommends it, add fluoride varnish (fluoride painted onto the teeth), sealants (which fill the grooves on the chewing surface), or a toothpaste for sensitive teeth. Which approach to use depends on the condition of each tooth, so decide together with the dentist.

Don't skip brushing because the teeth are sensitive. Even if it's gentle, brush every day with a soft brush and lukewarm water.

Frequently asked questions

Is a white patch a cavity?

Not necessarily. MIH patches are there from the moment the tooth comes in, and they have a different cause than cavities. But they're hard to tell apart by eye, and both can be present at once, so take a photo and have the dentist check.

Can the patches be made white?

Because the enamel itself formed softer, brushing won't remove them. Scrubbing hard can actually damage the weak spots. If patches on the front teeth bother you, talk with the dentist about options that suit the tooth.

Will other teeth be affected too?

MIH mostly shows up on the six-year molars and front teeth. If your child has patches, it's a good idea to look at the other teeth as well, so have the dentist check all the teeth at the checkup.

How often should my child have checkups?

Teeth with these patches do better with more frequent checkups, every 3 to 6 months. That way small changes can be found before a tooth chips or gets a cavity.

See the dentist promptly when

  • The chewing surface of a molar chips or breaks off
  • Cold water causes sharp pain that really bothers your child
  • Your child avoids chewing on one side
  • A patch darkens to brown or looks like it's hollowing out
  • A new molar or front tooth has uneven color and hasn't been checked by a dentist yet
