Why Do My Child's Teeth Look Black or Yellow? Common Causes of Stains

Kids' teeth change color for many reasons: black stain near the gums, iron, a fall, yellower permanent teeth, white spots. Plus why to skip home whitening.

At a glance

  • Black stain near the gums is harmless and cleans off
  • No home whitening, including charcoal or baking soda
  • Swollen gum, pus, pain, or fever? See a dentist right away

When you spot a black dot or line on your child's teeth while brushing, it's natural to worry it's a cavity. The same goes for a tooth that turns gray, or a new permanent tooth that looks extra yellow. Teeth change color for many reasons, and most can be sorted out with a dental visit. Here are the common causes and what to do at home.

Common reasons teeth change color

It's hard to tell a cavity from a stain at home. Still, knowing the common causes helps you worry less and makes the dentist's explanation easier to follow.

  • Black stain near the gums: Black dots or lines form in a row along the neck of the tooth near the gumline. It's linked to mouth bacteria and is harmless. It comes off with a professional cleaning, but it can come back. There are reports that kids with black stain tend to have fewer cavities. But whether the stain actually prevents cavities isn't known.
  • Iron supplement stain: Metals such as those in iron supplements stain the tooth surface. Because it's on the outside, it's relatively easy to remove at the dentist.
  • Discoloration after a fall: An injured baby tooth can turn gray, pink, or yellow. It may fade over weeks to months, and color change alone isn't a reason for root canal treatment. But it does need to be watched.
  • Permanent teeth looking more yellow: Baby teeth and permanent teeth have different enamel (outer layer) thickness. So during the changeover, a new permanent tooth can look more yellow than the baby tooth next to it, even in the same mouth.
  • White spots or streaks: This may be dental fluorosis, caused by swallowing too much fluoride while the permanent teeth are forming, up to about age 8. Most cases are mild.

Black stain used to be mistaken for a sign of poor brushing. Today it's seen as harmless, only a cosmetic concern. It's not your fault, so rest easy.

Don't scrape at home. Here's what to do

  1. Don't scrape or scrub black spots. Scraping with a fingernail or tool can damage the tooth surface or gums. Have them cleaned at the dentist.
  2. Have it taken care of at regular checkups. Black stain can come back, so have it checked at each visit and cleaned again if needed.
  3. Give iron syrup toward the back of the tongue. Using a straw or syringe to give it toward the back of the tongue keeps it off the front teeth. Afterward, offer water or brush the teeth.
  4. If a tooth changes color after a fall, keep a record. The dentist will take photos and follow it over time. Noting dates and taking photos at home also makes it easier to compare changes.
  5. Use only the right amount of fluoride toothpaste. That's a rice-grain smear under age 3, and a pea-sized amount from 3 to 6. Squeeze it out yourself, and have your child spit after brushing.

Don't whiten your child's teeth at home with whitening products, whitening strips, charcoal toothpaste, or baking soda scrubs. Charcoal toothpaste lacks evidence for effectiveness and safety, and harmful effects such as enamel wear have been reported.

Whitening the whole mouth while baby teeth and permanent teeth are mixed can leave the colors mismatched once all the permanent teeth come in. If your child's tooth color bothers you, the first step is to find out the cause at the dentist.

Frequently asked questions

Does a black spot mean a cavity?

Black dots in a row along the gumline may be a harmless stain. But cavities can also look brown or black, so it's hard to tell them apart at home. Have a dentist check.

We had it cleaned, and it came back.

Black stain can come back. It's not your child's fault, and it's harmless. Just have it checked at each regular visit and cleaned again if needed.

The new front teeth look really yellow.

Baby teeth and permanent teeth have different enamel thickness, so they can look different in color during the changeover. It's usually normal, but if you're worried, ask your dentist whether it's within the normal range.

Will iron supplements keep turning the teeth black?

Iron stains sit on the outside, so they're relatively easy to remove at the dentist. Giving it toward the back of the tongue and following with water or brushing helps. Talk with your pediatrician about whether to stop the iron.

When to see a dentist

  • The gum around a discolored tooth swells or a pimple-like opening appears
  • Your child has pain in a discolored tooth or a fever (go right away)
  • New black dots or brown stains appear on the teeth
  • A tooth changed color after a fall
  • You're worried because permanent teeth look especially yellow or have white spots or streaks
