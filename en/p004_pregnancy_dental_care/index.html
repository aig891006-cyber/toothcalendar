Is Dental Care Safe During Pregnancy? Checkups, Numbing, and X-Rays Explained

Dental checkups and needed treatment are safe in pregnancy. Why weeks 14 to 27 are easiest, plus numbing, X-rays, and what to tell the dental office.

At a glance

  • Check-ups and needed treatment are safe in pregnancy
  • Weeks 14–27 are the most comfortable time to go
  • X-rays only when truly needed, and the dose is very low

Once you find out you're pregnant, it can feel like the dentist should wait until after the baby comes. But checkups and needed treatment can be done during pregnancy, and putting them off is often the harder path. Here's when it's best to go, whether numbing and X-rays are okay, and what to tell the office at your visit.

The dentist is safe during pregnancy

Dental checkups, cleanings, fillings, and gum treatment are all safe during pregnancy. Hormone changes make gum inflammation more likely, and morning sickness or snacking habits can lead to cavities. Putting these problems off lets the inflammation grow until bigger treatment is needed, and the pain and stress are hard on both you and your baby.

The most comfortable time to go is the second trimester (weeks 14 to 27). Early on, morning sickness makes it hard to keep your mouth open. Late in pregnancy, a big belly makes it hard to lie back in the chair for long. In the second trimester, morning sickness has usually eased and your belly isn't big yet, so visits are easier. If you have treatment left to do, it's good to finish it during this time. Once your baby arrives, it gets much harder to find time for your own dental visits, so now is a great chance to take care of your mouth.

Local anesthesia can be used during pregnancy too. Getting treatment comfortably with numbing is easier on your body than enduring the pain.

X-rays are taken only when truly needed. Dental X-rays use a very low dose, so they can be taken during pregnancy if needed for a diagnosis. Current US guidance (ADA 2024) says lead aprons and thyroid collars aren't routinely needed, even during pregnancy. Some offices or countries still require them, so just follow your dentist's instructions.

Here's how to get ready for your visit

From booking to the visit itself, go through it in this order:

  1. When you book, tell the office that you're pregnant and how many weeks along you are.
  2. Bring a list of the medications and supplements you take. If your OB told you to be careful about anything, mention that too.
  3. If you need fillings, a cleaning, or gum treatment, don't put it off. Get it done within the second trimester.
  4. If an X-ray is recommended, feel free to ask why it's needed. Getting the X-rays you truly need keeps a diagnosis from being missed.
  5. If you're uncomfortable during treatment, raise your hand and ask for a short break.

As your belly grows, lying flat on your back can let the enlarged uterus press on a large blood vessel, making you dizzy or short of breath. If this happens, don't tough it out. Say so right away. Raising the chair a little or tilting your body slightly to your left side usually helps quickly.

A common mistake is putting up with a painful tooth "because I'm pregnant." Pain and swelling don't heal by waiting, and over time the treatment needed can get bigger.

Frequently asked questions

I'm in my first trimester and my tooth really hurts. Should I wait until the second trimester?

No. If you have pain or swelling, see a dentist right away, no matter how far along you are. Emergency care is possible at any stage of pregnancy.

Is dental numbing harmful to the baby?

Local anesthesia can be used during pregnancy. When you tell the office you're pregnant and how far along you are, they'll tailor your care to that.

They didn't put a lead apron on me. Is that okay?

Current US guidance says lead aprons and thyroid collars aren't routinely needed for dental X-rays, even during pregnancy. Rules differ between offices and countries, and some still use them, so if you're curious, just ask at the visit.

Can I get a cleaning too?

Yes, cleanings and gum treatment are safe during pregnancy. They're a big help in calming pregnancy gum inflammation.

I had dental X-rays before I knew I was pregnant.

Dental X-rays use a very low dose, and they're something that can be taken during pregnancy when needed. If it's still on your mind, let your OB know and talk it over.

When to see your dentist

  • A tooth hurts, or chewing is painful
  • Your gums keep swelling and bleeding
  • Your face or gums are swelling (go right away, no matter how far along you are)
  • You have an untreated cavity or treatment you've put off
  • You haven't had a checkup since becoming pregnant
