A Dental Plan for Mom and Baby After Birth: Postpartum Checkups, Breastfeeding, Cavities

Once the baby arrives, dentist time gets scarce. Plan your postpartum checkup, learn how breastfeeding relates to cavities, and prep baby's first visit.

At a glance

  • Plan your own dental check-up for after the birth
  • Breastfeeding is recommended. Breast milk alone doesn't cause cavities
  • Once teeth come in, clean them after night feeds too

As your due date gets closer, getting ready for the baby keeps pushing your own dental care down the list. But once the baby arrives, it gets much harder to find time for the dentist. Here's a dental plan for you and your baby that's good to set up now.

Why plan ahead around the birth?

Once the baby arrives, your days fill up with feeding, sleep, and diapers. It's not easy to set aside a few hours to go to the dentist on your own. That's why it helps to sort out any postponed treatment and your postpartum checkup plan now.

Gums that swelled during pregnancy usually settle within a few months after the birth. But cavities or gum inflammation that formed during pregnancy don't go away on their own, so once you've recovered, be sure to get a checkup.

If you're planning to breastfeed, you may be worried about cavities. Breastfeeding is recommended for your baby's health, and breast milk by itself doesn't cause cavities. But once teeth come in, feeding on demand all night without cleaning the teeth raises the risk of cavities. During sleep, saliva drops and can't wash leftover milk off the teeth as well. The point isn't to cut back on feeding. Once teeth come in, the key is cleaning them after feeds.

Here's how to plan for you and your baby

The plan doesn't need to be elaborate. Just writing two things on your calendar or phone, like "Mom's checkup" and "Baby's first dental visit," helps you remember.

Mom's plan

  1. Finish any treatment you can before the birth.
  2. Once you've recovered, book your own dental checkup and finish any postponed treatment.
  3. Dental treatment and local anesthesia are fine while breastfeeding. Tell the office you're nursing before treatment.
  4. Arranging ahead of time for someone to watch the baby during your visit makes booking much easier.

Baby's plan

  1. After birth, wipe the gums with damp gauze after feeds. Be sure to wipe after the last feed of the day.
  2. From the day the first tooth comes in, brush with a rice-grain smear of fluoride toothpaste, and clean the teeth after night feeds too.
  3. Plan the first dental visit before the first birthday. Going within 6 months of the first tooth is ideal.

If brushing after every night feed is hard, even one wipe of the teeth with damp gauze helps. If your sleepy baby won't open up, gently pull down the lower lip and start with the front teeth.

To wipe your baby's gums, wash your hands well, wrap gauze around your finger, and gently sweep the upper and lower gums and the tongue. It only takes a few seconds, so tie it to something you do every day, like burping after a feed, and you won't forget.

Trying to do all of this alone can wear you out. Dad or other family members can take over wiping the baby's gums or cleaning teeth at night. Dividing up the roles ahead of time also makes it easier to turn into a habit.

Frequently asked questions

Does breastfeeding make baby teeth decay easily?

Breast milk by itself doesn't cause cavities. But once teeth come in, feeding on demand all night without cleaning the teeth raises the risk of cavities. Once teeth come in, clean them after night feeds too.

I'm breastfeeding. Can I get dental numbing?

Yes, dental treatment and local anesthesia are possible while breastfeeding. Be sure to tell the office you're nursing before treatment and any prescriptions.

The gum lump from pregnancy is still there.

Gums that swelled during pregnancy usually settle within a few months after the birth. If the lump doesn't go away over time or the bleeding continues, have your dentist check it.

Do I have to stop night feeds once teeth come in?

That's not what it means. The key is cleaning the teeth after feeds. When and how to cut back can be talked over at the first dental visit, based on how your baby is doing.

What happens at a baby's first dental visit?

It's less about treatment and more about checking the mouth and teeth, and getting guidance on brushing and habits like feeding and snacks. It's also a good time to confirm the right toothpaste amount for your baby.

When to see the dentist

  • You've recovered from the birth but haven't had your own checkup yet
  • Gum bleeding continues after the birth, or a gum lump doesn't go away
  • You still have treatment left that was postponed during pregnancy
  • It's been 6 months since your baby's first tooth, or the first birthday is coming up
  • You see a white coating on your baby's gums or tongue that doesn't wipe away
