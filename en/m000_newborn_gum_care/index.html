Cleaning Baby's Gums Before Teeth: How to Start with Gauze

Even before teeth appear, wiping the gums with damp gauze after feeds helps. Why it matters, how often, step-by-step how-to, and tips for when your baby fusses.

At a glance

  • Wipe once or twice a day, especially after the last feed
  • Dampen the gauze with cooled boiled water
  • No toothpaste yet. Start it once the first tooth appears

You might wonder whether there's any point in cleaning your baby's mouth before a single tooth has shown up. Wiping the gums is easy, and it makes brushing much smoother later on. Here's why it helps, when and how to do it, and what to try when your baby doesn't like it.

Why wipe when there are no teeth yet

After a feed, a little milk stays behind in your baby's mouth. That leftover milk is food for the bacteria in the mouth. Bacteria start settling on the gums and tongue even before teeth come in, so wiping the gums after feeds helps keep the mouth clean.

The bigger reason is getting used to it. The mouth is a very sensitive place for a baby. If a toothbrush suddenly goes in on the day the first tooth appears, your baby may be startled and push back. Babies who are already used to a parent's touch inside the mouth resist the first brushing much less. Wiping the gums is really the first step toward a lifelong brushing habit.

Once or twice a day is plenty. You don't need to wipe after every feed, but do wipe after the last feed of the day. Saliva drops during sleep, so the mouth isn't rinsed as well overnight.

No toothpaste yet. Start a rice-grain smear of fluoride toothpaste once the first tooth appears. When that first tooth shows up, you can simply move from gauze to a finger brush or a baby toothbrush. The habit you've built will make that switch much easier.

A piece of gauze is all you need

You just need a clean gauze pad or soft cloth and some cooled boiled water.

  1. Wash your hands well with soap.
  2. Dampen the gauze or soft cloth with cooled boiled water and gently squeeze out the extra.
  3. Wrap the gauze once around your index finger.
  4. Lay your baby on your lap or cradle them in your arm with the head supported. You want a position where you can see the face and the head stays steady.
  5. Gently pull down the lower lip and softly sweep the lower gums from the inside out. Then wipe the upper gums the same way.
  6. Finally, lightly sweep the tongue from back to front once or twice.

You barely need to press. Think of it as a gentle sweep rather than a scrub. Going too far back on the tongue can make your baby gag, so only wipe as far as you can see.

Watch out for a couple of common mistakes. Some parents scrub hard to rub off something white, or use salt water or other cleansers to get things extra clean. A baby's gums are very tender, and gently wiping with plain water is all they need.

It's cleanest to use a fresh piece of gauze each time.

It's okay if your baby turns away or cries. Sing or talk to your baby, keep it short, and do it at the same time every day so it starts to feel familiar. Tie it to something you already do every day, like burping after a feed or finishing bath time, so it doesn't slip your mind.

Frequently asked questions

When should I start?

You can start any time after your baby is born. The earlier you start, the more naturally your baby gets used to having the inside of the mouth touched.

Do I need to wipe after every feed?

No, once or twice a day is enough. But it's a good idea to wipe after the last feed of the day.

Can I use tap water?

For wiping the gums before teeth come in, cooled boiled water is recommended. Test it on your wrist to make sure it has cooled to lukewarm.

There's something white on the tongue. Do I have to wipe it off?

If it comes off easily with damp gauze, it's milk residue. If it's a white coating that won't wipe off, don't scrub. Have a pediatrician or dentist take a look.

My baby really hates it. Can I stop?

It's natural for babies to dislike it at first. Even just a few seconds is fine, so keep going with the same routine every day. Once your baby is used to it, the first brushing will go much more smoothly too.

When to see a dentist

  • A white coating on the gums or tongue stays even after wiping
  • The gums are swollen and your baby refuses to feed
  • A bump-like swelling on the gums gets in the way of feeding
  • Your baby was born with a tooth
  • Once the first tooth comes in, plan the first dental visit within 6 months and no later than the first birthday
